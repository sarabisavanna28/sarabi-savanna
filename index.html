The home of safari

Kenya, from the Rift Valley to the Indian Ocean — and across the border into northern Tanzania. Choose a region, or let us combine several into one seamless journey.

Kenya

A land of astounding diversity

Straddling the equator between the Rift Valley and the Indian Ocean, Kenya is a land of remarkable geographical, cultural and ecological diversity. Known as the birthplace of safari, it rewards travellers like nowhere else.

Maasai Mara

South-west KenyaMigration Jul – OctBig Five

Kenya’s most iconic landscape: savanna plains punctuated by acacia trees and teeming with wildlife. During the Great Migration, over two million wildebeest surge into the Mara from Tanzania, creating a spectacle like no other. Camps in the community-owned conservancies bordering the reserve offer greater freedom and exclusivity, while directly supporting Maasai livelihoods.

  • Mara River crossings
  • Hot air balloon at sunrise
  • Maasai village visits
  • Private conservancies

Amboseli & Chyulu

Southern KenyaElephant herdsKilimanjaro views

Journey south-east to Amboseli, where great herds of elephants roam against the stunning white backdrop of Mount Kilimanjaro. From there, explore the lush hills and forests of Chyulu — green, volcanic and quiet.

  • Kilimanjaro sundowners
  • Elephant photography
  • Chyulu Hills

Laikipia, Ol Pejeta & Mount Kenya

Central KenyaRhino sanctuaryYear-round

North of Nairobi, at the base of Mount Kenya, the plains of Laikipia were once dominated by cattle ranches and have transformed into a conservation haven sheltering rhino, African wild dog and giraffe. Exclusive lodges here go beyond the game drive: bush walks, camel treks, horseback safaris and night drives, with Ol Pejeta’s rhino sanctuary and chimpanzee sanctuary close at hand.

  • Ol Pejeta Conservancy
  • Bush walks & night drives
  • Mount Kenya treks
  • Meru National Park

Samburu & the Northern Frontier

Northern KenyaCultureExpedition

Further north, the land shifts to rugged, khaki-toned terrain — home to the Samburu people, their rich culture and inspiring community-led conservation. Beyond lie Kenya’s least-visited regions: Marsabit’s forested highlands, the Chalbi Desert and Lake Turkana, the Jade Sea.

  • Samburu National Reserve
  • Marsabit & Lake Paradise
  • Chalbi Desert
  • Lake Turkana

The Rift Valley & Tsavo

Central & south-eastLakesRed-earth elephants

The Rift Valley unfolds with its salty lakes, deserts and volcanic landscapes — Lake Nakuru’s flamingos among them — best explored by helicopter for a breathtaking, bird’s-eye view of Kenya’s untamed beauty. To the south-east, Tsavo’s vast wilderness holds Mzima Springs, the Shetani lava flows and its famous red-dust elephants.

  • Lake Nakuru
  • Helicopter safaris
  • Tsavo East & West

The Coast

Indian OceanDiani · Watamu · MalindiBush & beach

Trade dust for white sand. Diani’s beaches and the marine park at Kisite Mpunguti, dhow sails off Wasini Island, Watamu’s reefs and Mida Creek, and the Swahili heritage of Malindi and the Gede ruins. The perfect final chapter after the bush.

  • Diani Beach
  • Wasini & Kisite Mpunguti
  • Watamu Marine Park
  • Shimba Hills
Tanzania

Where the migration begins

The Serengeti’s endless plains and the Ngorongoro Crater lie just across the border. We craft Tanzania journeys and Kenya–Tanzania combinations on request — talk to us about the right season for your route.

Serengeti

Northern TanzaniaGreat Migration

Endless grassland under a vast sky, where the wildebeest begin their year-long loop before crossing into the Mara. Combine it with Kenya for the migration from both sides.

Ngorongoro

Crater highlandsYear-round

A collapsed volcano whose forested rim rises above a crater floor thick with wildlife. Misty mornings, extraordinary density, and a natural pairing with the Serengeti and Kilimanjaro.

Jan – Feb

Green season

28°avg · ~100 mm

Warm and largely dry; lush landscapes and excellent light. A good time for the coast, too.

Mar – May

Long rains

26°avg · ~170 mm

Dramatic skies, fewer visitors and quieter camps — a photographer’s season.

Jun – Sep

Dry season

25°avg · ~82 mm

The most popular time for safari: cooler, drier and coinciding with the migration’s arrival on the Mara.

Oct – Dec

Short rains

27°avg · ~125 mm

Brief afternoon showers, green plains and newborn wildlife as the year turns.

Kenya seasonal averages. The most popular months are late June to October, but every season has its own rewards — we’ll advise on the best timing for your route.

Our founder & her story

Kenya beyond the postcards

Sarabi Savanna is a proudly female-owned business, founded by someone with a deep-rooted love for Kenya and its breathtaking landscapes. Growing up, her family — especially her father — instilled a passion for safaris, with countless trips across the country, from the famous Maasai Mara to hidden gems few visitors ever see. Back then, it was simply home; something so familiar it was easy to take for granted.

“Many people didn’t see Kenya the way I did. Their view was often limited or overshadowed by misconceptions.”

Everything changed with a move to the UK. Travelling widely and meeting people from all over the world revealed something surprising: Kenya’s vibrancy, its culture and the warmth of its people were largely unknown. That realisation sparked Sarabi Savanna — a company founded to share the Kenya she knows and loves with the world.

Whether it’s the vast, wildlife-rich plains of the Maasai Mara, the rhino sanctuaries of Ol Pejeta, the rugged beauty of Laikipia or corners of the country few get to experience, Sarabi Savanna is here to open up Kenya’s magic to you. Every traveller is different, and so is every safari we create.
